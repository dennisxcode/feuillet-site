Three things, not one

A project is not a big task.

An assignment, a project and an exam fail in completely different ways, so Feuillet treats them as three types. What separates them is how much the app legitimately knows about your work.

Assignment

It has a due date, and the date is the point.

Lands on its class period, counts down, and turns up in the morning list on the day it is due. Late work stays visible instead of scrolling away.

What the app knows: when it is due.

Project

Assigned three weeks out, finished the night before.

Feuillet will not propose a plan for this. It has no idea what the project is or who else is on it. It keeps the thing visible and tells you how long it has been sitting, and that is all.

What the app knows: almost nothing, and it says so by staying quiet.

Exam

The one place it is allowed to have opinions.

You answer a few questions once: subject, chapters, and your own estimate of the study time you need. From that it paces reminders backwards from the date, so the night before is never the first one.

What the app knows: what you told it, which is enough to be useful.

Not started Started In progress Blocked Done

Feuillet tracks how long something has sat in a state, not just which state it is in. Marking a project "in progress" and forgetting about it is the exact move this is built to catch, so the oldest stalled thing comes back on a widening interval until it moves.

The Mac app

It lives in the notch.

A real app, not a browser tab wearing a hat. Its own window, its own menu, and a panel that hangs out of the notch above whatever you are working in. It floats over fullscreen apps and never steals focus, so it is there while you are writing the essay it is reminding you about.

  • Tick without opening anything. The next three things due have checkboxes right there. A tick is written immediately, the row stays struck through, and you can undo for a few seconds before it fades.
  • You choose what is on it. Up next, grades, your tree, a study timer counting down in the strip, a line you jotted. Twenty eight settings, and a cog on the panel that opens the right one.
  • It is also a mirror. Press the small button and the board blurs out, the panel reshapes to the picture and fills itself with your camera. The light only ever comes on when you press it.
  • Nothing on it opens the app. Except one arrow, in the footer, on purpose. A miss between two rows should not pull a window over your work.

Every mark says how old it is

Only your Mac refreshes from the portal, and every other device shows the result. So each figure carries the moment it was read. A stale grade presented as current is worse than no grade.

Nothing is invented to fill a gap

Work that has been handed in but not marked is left out of the average rather than counted as zero. If your school has not published a section yet, the screen says so instead of computing a number from nothing.

Your password never leaves your Mac

It is encrypted into the macOS keychain on your own machine and is never sent to Feuillet. What syncs to your phone is the arithmetic, not the login.

Built for COBA, the portal Collège Jean de Brébeuf runs, which is the same system several other Quebec schools use. It cannot be switched on until a term is publishing marks, so it is listed as Soon rather than shown as working.

Connect what you already use

Migrate, do not add a sixth tab.

The point is not to be an extension of your other apps. It is that everything ends up here, so there is one place to look. What comes in becomes an ordinary item on the board, and the calendar, the panel and tomorrow's reminder all cover it without caring where it came from.

Reminders

Four kinds, and never one per task.

Five things due Friday is a fact about Friday, so it is one notification about Friday. Nothing here fires because an app wants attention. Every one of them is switchable, and the times are yours.

Notifications are asked for once, and only with your permission. Nothing fires while the app is fully quit or the tab is closed, which is stated here because it is true.

There is no chat box.

Feuillet does not want to have a conversation with you. There is nothing to prompt and nothing to ask. The clever parts are the reminder that arrives at the right moment, the handwriting that turns into next Tuesday, and the warning when a subject starts slipping. That work happens underneath, and then gets out of the way.

Finishing things

A checkbox is a poor reward.Soon

Every finished thing earns a leaf, and the leaves grow a tree that is yours and keeps growing all year. Rewarding beats guilt, which is why there is no streak to break and nothing turns red because you had a bad week.

The leaf is already the mark over the i in Feuillet's own name, and already what you get when you tick something. The tree it grows into is the part still being designed.
